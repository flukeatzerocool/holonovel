# Dungeon World SRD — Vendor Enrichment

Source: Sage LaTorra and Adam Koebel, CC-BY 3.0 Unported
URL: https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com
Confidence: HIGH (curated, licensed SRD)

Dungeon World's GM chapter presents its agenda, principles, and moves as rules,
not advice. These items paraphrase that procedure and the Fronts/Dangers
structure so the GM surface can offer a codified play loop and campaign
planning model alongside the other vendor sources.

## Module: supplementary_guidance (4 items)

### `dw-gm-agenda` — HIGH

The GM has three goals and no others: portray a fantastic world, fill the
characters' lives with adventure, and play to find out what happens. The agenda
is a rule, not a preference — it rules out trying to beat the players, testing
their puzzle-solving, showing off a pre-written setting, or telling a planned
story. Adventures portray a setting in motion; they never presume player
actions. Maps to Holonovel's narrative directives (REQ-081) and world
in motion (REQ-233a) without overriding them.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, The GM — Agenda

### `dw-gm-principles` — HIGH

Twelve guides keep the GM on the agenda: draw maps and leave blanks; address
the characters, not the players; embrace the fantastic; make a move that
follows; never speak the name of your move; give every monster life; name every
person; ask questions and use the answers; be a fan of the characters; think
dangerous; begin and end with the fiction; think offscreen too. Use them as a
stance checklist for any scene, not as a second rule system.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, The GM — Principles

### `dw-gm-moves` — HIGH

The GM's moment-to-moment menu: use a monster, danger, or location move; reveal
an unwelcome truth; show signs of an approaching threat; deal damage; use up
their resources; turn their move back on them; separate them; give an
opportunity that fits a class's abilities; show a downside to their class, race,
or equipment; offer an opportunity with or without cost; put someone in a spot;
tell them the requirements or consequences and ask. Make a soft move when the
table looks to the GM; make a hard move when they give a golden opportunity or
roll a 6−. A soft move ignored becomes a golden opportunity for a hard move.
Always end with "What do you do?" Maps to consequence suggestion and decision
workflows, not to an automatic state mutation.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, The GM — Moves

### `dw-play-loop` — HIGH

The loop is fiction-first: describe the immediate situation in concrete sensory
detail, let the players describe what their characters do, and treat a move as
triggered by that fiction rather than named at the table. Resolve with the
move's own bands — 10+ complete success, 7–9 partial success, 6− a GM move —
then return to the fiction and ask "What do you do?" Never announce the move's
name; show its result as a straightforward outcome of the characters' action.
Maps to Holonovel's parser floor plus GM narration, not to a replacement
resolution engine.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, The GM — How to GM; Playing the Game

## Module: adventure_advice (4 items)

### `dw-fronts` — HIGH

A front is a collection of linked dangers plus one or more impending dooms. The
**adventure front** covers the current problem across a few sessions; the
**campaign front** unifies the campaign with slower, larger portents. Create
2–3 dangers per front, choose an impending doom for each, add 1–3 grim portents
for an adventure front (3–5 for a campaign front), write 1–3 stakes questions,
and list the cast. Leave blanks for later discovery. Maps to Holonovel's
adventure and faction structures without replacing them.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, Fronts and Dangers

### `dw-danger-types` — HIGH

Every danger has an impulse — the motivation that drives it toward its doom.
Five families organize them: **Ambitious Organizations** (to absorb power and
grow), **Planar Forces** (to fulfill a cosmic impulse), **Arcane Enemies** (to
seek power, immortality, or a worthy wielder), **Hordes** (to grow, spread, and
consume), and **Cursed Places** (to corrupt, lure, or disgorge). Each family
carries its own move list. Use the type and impulse as a compact NPC/faction
brief.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, Fronts and Dangers — Types of Dangers

### `dw-grim-portents` — HIGH

Grim portents are the danger's dark plan if no one intervenes; advance them
descriptively (the change happened in play) or prescriptively (as a hard move).
When all portents of a danger come to pass, its **impending doom** sets in:
Tyranny, Pestilence, Destruction, Usurpation, Impoverishment, or Rampant Chaos.
The world changes even when a danger is stopped, if any portent was fulfilled.
Maps to countdown/danger clocks (REQ-073), with the portent chain as the
narrative explanation of each tick.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, Fronts and Dangers — Grim Portents; Impending Doom

### `dw-front-resolution` — HIGH

Resolve a front by defeating, turning, or surviving its dangers; then inspect
the aftermath. Move unresolved dangers up to the campaign front, promote or
demote survivors, and let the outcome seed the next adventure front. Multiple
adventure fronts run in parallel; neglected fronts advance offscreen. Stakes
questions are 1–3 concrete questions about people, places, or groups that the GM
genuinely wants resolved through play — once written, they are out of the GM's
hands.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, Fronts and Dangers — Resolving a Front; Stakes

## Module: lore_templates (2 items)

### `dw-danger-archetypes` — HIGH

| Family | Example dangers | Impulse |
|---|---|---|
| Ambitious Organizations | Cabal, Cult, Thieves Guild, Corrupt Government | to absorb those in power, to grow; to infest from within; to take by subterfuge; to maintain the status quo |
| Planar Forces | God, Demon Prince, Choir of Angels, Construct of Law | to gather worshipers; to open the gates of Hell; to pass judgment; to eliminate perceived disorder |
| Arcane Enemies | Lord of the Undead, Power-mad Wizard, Sentient Artifact, Dragon | to seek true immortality; to seek magical power; to find a worthy wielder; to hoard and protect |
| Hordes | Wandering Barbarians, Humanoid Vermin, Plague of the Undead | to grow strong; to breed and consume; to spread |
| Cursed Places | Dark Portal, Shadowland, Unholy Ground, Place of Power | to disgorge demons; to corrupt the living; to spawn evil; to be controlled or tamed |

Use as a seed table for a faction, location, or threat with a built-in motive.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, Fronts and Dangers — Types of Dangers

### `dw-front-template` — MEDIUM

Front: `<name>`. Kind: campaign or adventure. Dangers: 2–3, each with a type,
an impulse, 1–3 (adventure) or 3–5 (campaign) grim portents, and one impending
doom. Stakes: 1–3 concrete questions about people, places, or groups. Cast:
named people and creatures with a detail each. Aftermath: which portents came
to pass, who moved, and what the next front inherits.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, Fronts and Dangers — Creating Fronts

## Module: narrative_voices (2 items)

### `dw-monster-life` — HIGH

Give every monster a life: a motivation, a sensory signature, and a detail that
makes it real. Nothing the GM creates is protected — everything can be put in
danger, destroyed, or changed. Think dangerous: the world changes for the worse
without the characters' intervention. This is a GM stance for portraying
threats, not a stat block.

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, The GM — Principles (Give every monster life; Think dangerous)

### `dw-name-every-person` — HIGH

Anyone the characters speak with has a name, and probably a personality, a goal,
and an opinion. Start with the name and let the rest flow. NPCs are people with
their own goals and the means to pursue them; they remember how the characters
treated them, and they change over time. Maps to Holonovel's named-NPC and
campaign-memory surfaces (REQ-075, REQ-310).

Source anchor: Dungeon World SRD, The GM — Principles; Dealing With Common Situations — People
