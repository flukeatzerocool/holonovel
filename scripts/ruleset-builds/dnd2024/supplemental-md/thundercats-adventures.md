## Introduction

INTRODUCTION ThunderCats Adventures From beyond any known galaxy, bringing with them the laws and ideals of their doomed planet, Thundera, come the ThunderCats! An orchestral sting rises, followed by the thunder of a reverberating gong as lightning tears across the darkened sky above the Onyx Pyramid of Third Earth. Within its shadowed halls, lit by a sickly glow, the mummified sorcerer Mumm-Ra peers into his bubbling cauldron while Ma-Mutt snarls at his side. The swirling image reveals his endless torment, the ThunderCats, charging into yet another adventure. The devil-priest trembles with rage, lifting his withered arms as dark power gathers and a new diabolical scheme takes shape. This is how many memorable adventures within the action-packed world of ThunderCats begin. Ever since its debut in 1987, the stories of ThunderCats have captured the imagination of its fans, and this book is a celebration of these captivating tales. Each of the five adventures presented here are intended to immerse players into the world of ThunderCats with a refreshing modern twist. Designed for characters of levels 3 through 12, the adventures unfold across the different eras of the ThunderCats saga, allowing players to experience the full gambit of what ThunderCats has to offer. Wherever evil exists, Mumm-Ra lives, and the ThunderCats must answer the call!

### Using This Book

The adventures in this anthology are designed for use alongside the ThunderCats 5.5e RPG Campaign and Setting Guide and use the Fifth Edition ruleset along with the additional options presented in that guide. To get the most out of this book, you will need the Fifth Edition core rules and the ThunderCats 5.5e RPG Campaign and Setting Guide. These adventures are versatile resources that can fill a variety of needs at your gaming table: as one-shot adventures, as “filler episodes” in an ongoing campaign, or as a series that forms a campaign, bringing characters from level 3 to a climactic conclusion at level 12. These adventures are designed to require minimal preparation (see Running the Adventures later in this introduction). Although written for ThunderCats, these adventures can be adapted to other campaign settings with only minor adjustments. A few renamed locations, a handful of altered details, and a fresh thematic layer are often all it takes to make them feel at home in your own world.

### One-Shot Adventure

Maybe you’re usually a player in a long-running campaign and want to try sitting in the Game Master chair. Maybe you’re assembling a new group and want to see how everyone clicks before committing to a full campaign. Maybe your regular ThunderCats GM is out sick or caught in a scheduling snare, but the rest of the table is ready for action. Or maybe your group just needs a ThunderCats themed detour from the ongoing story. Whatever the reason, you can choose an adventure from this book, gather your heroes, and pounce into the fray!

### In an Ongoing Campaign

Maybe your players’ characters know what they need to do to stop the evil boss, but they are not quite high enough level to succeed. Maybe they have just reached a major milestone and you want to give them a change of pace before diving into the next act of the story. Maybe they are traveling halfway across the world and you want to keep the journey interesting with a small adventure along the way. Or maybe you are not prepared for the next main adventure and need a quick diversion. In any of these cases, you can choose an adventure from this book to serve as a side quest or filler episode in your ongoing campaign. When the characters are at the right level for the adventure, simply explain how they end up at its starting point. You can adjust the details at the beginning of an adventure to fit your campaign while keeping the core concepts included. If you have time to prepare, you can weave the adventure into the themes and challenges of your campaign. Rename people and places to match your world, connect the adventure villains to key organizations or events, and hide clues to your campaign’s story within the rewards.

### Creating a Campaign

These adventures can be combined into a full ThunderCats campaign. Guide your players through the adventures in the order presented and award story-based level advancement at the end of each one so characters are ready for the next challenge. You can also use the following narrative framework to link the adventures together. The characters begin as part of the exodus from Thundera aboard the Thunderian flagship when it crash-lands on Third Earth. They are immediately thrust into the dangers of this new world while encountering its curious native inhabitants. After rejoining the ThunderCats, the characters embark on many missions across Third Earth. One adventure takes them to Thorburn, where they face the Berserkers and manage to fend them off before being drawn into an urgent mission. When Thundera is restored, the characters join the hunt for the pieces of the Treasures of Thundera, sent to retrieve one from the Valley of the Snarfs before getting embroiled in a plot by Mumm-Ra to claim it first. After the mu’tants and Lunataks have been banished from Third Earth and the rest of the ThunderCats have left for New Thundera, the characters remain on Third Earth while to help the League of Third Earth wrap up the last Berserker raids along the shorelines. Finally, the characters join the ThunderCats on New Thundera and find themselves the planet’s last hope as Mumm-Ra makes one final effort to crush them, forcing the characters to invade his lair and defeat him once and for all.

### Running the Adventures

As mentioned in “Using This Book”, you need the Fifth Edition core rules and the ThunderCats 5.5e RPG Campaign and Setting Guide to run each of these adventures. Spells, equipment, magic items, and stat blocks mentioned in the adventure are described in the Fifth Edition core rules unless otherwise specified, in which case they either will be from the ThunderCats 5.5e RPG Campaign and Setting Guide or included in this book. The table of contents summarizes the adventures in this anthology. Each adventure is designed for four to six characters of a particular level, but you can adjust for larger or smaller groups by changing the number of foes in an encounter and giving a small group additional resources. Text that appears in a box like this is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased for the players when their characters first arrive at a location or under a specific circumstance as described in the text. When a creature’s name appears in bold type, that’s a visual cue pointing you to its stat block.

### Network–Approved

Before starting an adventure with a new group of players, have an open discussion about any topics or themes that should be avoided during gameplay. Players may have phobias or triggers that you aren’t aware of, so it’s important to establish boundaries. Any content that makes a player feel unsafe should be excluded. If a topic or theme makes someone uneasy but they consent to include it, handle it with care and be prepared to move on quickly if it becomes uncomfortable. While characters can experience stress and anxiety, your players should always feel safe and have fun.

PLAYING AS THE THUNDERCATS The adventures in this book assume the player characters are aspiring ThunderCats, or at least not yet members of the core team. If your players prefer to take on the roles of the well-known ThunderCats, you can certainly accomplish this with some fairly minor adjustments to the stories.

### Adventure Background

As Jaga the Wise led the Thunderian refugees through space, they watched their homeworld Thundera explode. Their grief was interrupted when a coalition of enemy Plun-Darr ships attacked to seize the Sword of Omens. Amid fierce fighting, Jaga’s ship survived, and the young lord, Lion-O, awakened the sword’s power to repel the invaders. The Plun-Darr mu’tants pursued Jaga, whose damaged ship could only reach a remote planet called Third Earth. Jaga manually piloted the ship while the Thunderians entered suspension capsules, knowing he might not survive. Years later, as Third Earth came into view, Jaga’s body failed. He set the ship on autopilot and transferred his essence to the Eye of Thundera. The ship crash-landed in the northern hemisphere, scattering the suspension capsules across the land. As Lion-O and his team awaken and battle the mu’tants, other Thunderians awaken miles from the crash site near the scavenger village of Bridgewater, where a different and unexpected threat closes in. Their capsules broke into two groups. One group is discovered by the Children of the Enchanted Dawn, a cult of cat-worshippers who mistake the Thunderians for children of their deity, all-father Aegeanian. Their prophecy commands a sacred ritual, for they believe that by consuming the life force of the Thunderians they will claim immortality as eternal warriors. ### Bridgewater

Bridgewater Built mostly from scrap metal, the village of Bridgewater is home to a community of scavengers. Its people survive on Third Earth by exploring ancient First Earth technological sites and turning whatever they find into usable building materials. They understand little of the technology itself, valuing it more so for the metal and parts it provides. Unlike most settlements on Third Earth, which are dominated by a single species, Bridgewater welcomes a wide variety of inhabitants, as well as diverse travelers. This mix of cultures has strengthened the town and made its salvage-based way of life more successful. Life in Bridgewater runs on shared effort and mutual support. Every inhabitant contributes what they can, be it labor, skills, or resources, and what is gathered or built is available to all who need it. Decisions are made together, with the input of everyone involved, and no single individual controls the village’s wealth or direction. Success and failure are experienced collectively, and the survival of the town depends on this cooperative spirit as much as on scavenged metal or salvaged tech. The nearby crashing of the Thunderian suspension capsules has caught the attention of the village, so the town has sent a few trusted and capable individuals to investigate. They are to see if anything of value can be recovered and, if there are survivors, provide what help they can while gathering any knowledge possible from the aliens.

### the Enchanted Dawn

The woods north of Bridgewater hide a cult called the Children of the Enchanted Dawn. They worship the all-father Aegeanian, following his scripture and surrendering themselves completely. His teachings guide them to cleanse the world of sin and rot, so they might one day become everlasting, free from mortal concerns like disease and death. The people of Bridgewater fear the cult. Stories say they use poisoned darts and drag their victims to a hidden underground temple. There, the cult inflicts harsh punishments, believing that sins can only be cleansed through pain and suffering. When a victim finally shows repentance, they are offered to their god in a blazing ritual. The cult believed their prayers had been answered when a gift from the heavens fell to Third Earth, just as Aegeanian had foretold: “On the eve of the endtimes, I shall give my flesh unto you so that you may know me. So that you will be cleansed by my blood and join by my side in everlasting life. And in everlasting life, shall you carry my message and march forth as my soldiers, cleansing the soil of sin.” When the Thunderians emerged from their suspension capsules, the cult struck with poison-laced darts and captured them. Now the Thunderians lie in the cult’s underground temple as preparations begin for a ritual where the Thunderians will serve as the “guests of honor.”

### Exploring Bridgewater

Bridgewater is a chaotic patchwork of metal sheets, wooden planks, and scavenged tech parts, forming ramshackle homes, workshops, and marketplaces. Wires snake across rooftops, pulleys and cranes dangle salvaged components, and signs crafted from bent metal announce each establishment. Despite its disorganized appearance, the town hums with life. Fires smolder in cook pits, scavenger crews repair broken machinery, and traders haggle over ancient tech. There is some level of electricity available through solar power, but thundrillium technology (see the ThunderCats 5.5e RPG Campaign and Setting Guide) is a rarity. See “Bridgewater” in the “Adventure Background” section for more information.

### Area Information

Bridgewater has the following features. Dimensions and Terrain. Bridgewater’s buildings sprawl outward, tightly packed and often rising in multiple levels. Piles of scrap metal, rubble, and old vehicle parts create natural barriers and vantage points. In the distance, the northern woods rise like a dark, looming border. Lighting. Sunlight strikes the metal surfaces and glints sharply, sending blinding flashes through certain areas. Smells and Sounds. The air smells of smoke, cooking fires, and oiled metal with hints of burned wiring, trash, and damp wood. Noise fills every corner as hammers strike, machinery whirs, traders shout, animals screech, and the constant murmur of life echoes off metal sheets.

### Moving Quickly

If the characters decide to push through the woods at a faster pace, they can make up valuable time. However, haste comes at a cost. While traveling quickly, the characters have Disadvantage on Perception checks, reducing their Passive Perceptions by 5. In exchange for this increased risk, add 1 to the time limit (see “Clock’s Ticking” earlier in this adventure).

### Moving With Caution

The characters may choose to travel through the woods quietly, particularly if they know about the Children of the Enchanted Dawn. To do so, a character makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check against the cultists’ Passive Perception. The cultists have Advantage because the forest is their home and gain a +5 bonus, setting their Passive Perception at 19. Doing this slows the characters down, however, subtracting 1 from the time limit (see “Clock’s Ticking” earlier in this adventure).

### Ruins

Following the tracks leads to the Ruins. The Enchanted Dawn Temple rests underground in the long-abandoned basement of these ancient ruins. Above, crumbling stone and rusted metal stretch across the overgrown woods. The only structure that seems functional is a water mill with its metal wheel spinning in the river that winds through the forest.

### Area Information

The Ruins have the following features. Dimensions and Terrain. The area is uneven, littered with toppled walls, shattered columns, and twisted metal frameworks. A deep rushing river cuts through the site, dividing it. Thick undergrowth fills the spaces between structures, creating patches of Difficult Terrain. Lighting. Sunlight filters sporadically through gaps in the crumbling roofs and treetops, casting dappled shadows across mossy stone and rusted iron, giving the ruins a perpetual Dim Light. Smells and Sounds. The air is sharp with the tang of rust and wet stone, mixed with the earthy scent of moss and damp leaves. Water from the river churns softly against the wheel while distant creaks of metal and the occasional rustle of unseen wildlife punctuate the silence.

### Cult Patrol

Patrolling the Ruins are two Enchanted Dawn Cultists for every character. They will attack on sight, but may be fooled by characters wearing the green cloaks from Enchanted Dawn Ambushers (see the “Traps and Ambush” in the “The Woods” section earlier in this adventure) and a successful DC 13 Charisma (Deception) check. Retreat. If over half of their number are defeated, the remaining cultists will attempt to flee and alert the temple. Any cultist who escapes puts the temple on High Alert, if it wasn’t already (see further in this adventure).

### High Alert

If the temple is on High Alert (see “Traps and Ambush” in the “The Woods” section earlier in this adventure), the patrolling cultists are hiding in the water mill and temple entrance (see below), waiting for the characters to enter either area so they can gang up and attack them. Whichever area engages combat first will call for the other to join. They will be highly suspicious of anyone dressed in the ambushers’ cloaks, gaining Advantage on Insight checks against any attempts at deception.

### Temple Entrance

It isn’t too difficult to find the temple entrance, as it is within the only ruined building cleared of overgrowth, roughly 60 feet away from the water mill. Password. The entrance to the temple is a sturdy wooden door with a metal plate at roughly eye level for a Medium creature. Cultists usually enter by knocking on the door, waiting for someone on the other side to slide the metal plate, and then speaking the password (“Aegeanian”). Observant characters can learn this by waiting and watching the patrolling cultists (see ”Cult Patrol”) and succeeding on a DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check, but doing so will take time and subtract 1 from the time limit (see “Clock’s Ticking” earlier in this adventure). Door. The wooden door is fitted with a simple lock of good quality (1 action to pick, DC 15). A cultist who notices someone trying to pick the lock will assume that person is an intruder.

### Water Mill

The only building of interest in the Ruins is the Water Mill. This mill has been repaired by the Children of the Enchanted Dawn and its metal wheel is used as a small-scale hydroelectric generator to power their temple. Sabotage. A character can stop the water mill from generating electricity by tampering with the wheel. Any method that makes sense is likely to work, but the patrolling cultists (see earlier in this adventure) will see any activity around the water mill as suspicious or hostile. Once the sabotage succeeds, attention will quickly turn to the mill, even if the patrolling cultists are no longer around, and any characters present will be questioned or attacked by another patrol. The mill’s shutdown will also cut power to the Enchanted Dawn Temple (see “Enchanted Dawn Temple” further in this adventure).

### Conclusion

Once the Thunderians are rescued from the Enchanted Dawn Temple, they express deep gratitude toward their rescuers. Thunderian characters may return to their crash site and begin the search for Lion-O and the rest of their scattered people, while characters native to Third Earth have the opportunity to forge an alliance with the newcomers and gain access to new technologies and valuable knowledge to aid Bridgewater. Ewebert, too, is incredibly grateful for his rescue and promises the characters lifelong discounts on all his wares. If this adventure serves as part of an ongoing campaign, the characters may choose to finish exploring the temple, claim any remaining treasure, and eliminate lingering cultists before departing. For a self-contained one-shot, however, the rescue of the Thunderians marks the natural end of the adventure. ### Adventure Background

### The Adventure Begins

Knowing the dangers of carrying packages through Thorburn, the bolkin courier Franket Roadsworth, owner of Roadsworthy Transit, hides his deliveries in an old Pro-Tech 99 storage vault system a day outside the town of Baddeck. He sometimes uses these vaults to keep items or even himself safe. He has recently stashed a new package meant for Willa and her warrior maidens before continuing toward Baddeck. However, it seems that Franket may have said too much at the saloon about this delivery. A group of drunken Berserkers named Cruncher, Top Spinner, and Ram-Bam took notice and followed him with the intention of stealing the package. Franket was heading toward the vault when he spotted the Berserkers on his trail. He fell back on a traditional bolkin evasion tactic of running in completely random, panicked directions. Now he finds himself under attack in an unfamiliar part of Thorburn, and he only knows how to get to the vault using landmarks noted from Baddeck. This package is of the utmost importance, as it contains a live ebon unicorn foal whose mother was lost in a recent raid. The foal must reach Willa’s sanctuary in the Unicorn Forest to be protected and to potentially draw other rare unicorns to the area, increasing their safety against poachers. The crate was carefully enchanted and provisioned by the Circle of the Croft unicorn experts. It has kept the foal warm, fed, and safe for days, but the druids’ magic is starting to fade. Franket’s hasty flight in the wrong direction may have put the precious creature in a new kind of danger. With the Berserkers closing in, Franket is running out of time. The crate’s magic will not last much longer, and against foes like the Berserkers, the lone bolkin stands little chance without aid. The player characters are travelling to or from nearly any biome on Third Earth and find themselves in the Thorburn dust grotto area. However, much like the other important environments in ThunderCats, the exact location is less important than the exciting and adventurous events happening there. As they travel, the terrain slowly shifts to a rockier and dustier environment surrounded on many sides by high, flat plateaus and scattered cliffs. As the transition continues, any character who succeeds on a DC 14 Intelligence (History or Nature) check recognizes one or more of the following hints that they have indeed entered (or are entering) Thorburn. • The ground is much warmer than it ought to be due to underground volcanic activity. • There is a high amount of salt in the dust and air. It can be tasted when breathed, and it stings the eyes and open wounds. • Even small sounds reverberate somewhat off the cliff walls, making it both difficult to move silently and track anything using primarily their sense of hearing. • Thunderians can feel the presence of thundrainium when close to the cliffs, but not enough to be adversely affected by it. • If the characters are travelling by vehicle, the rare metals and minerals throughout Thorburn cause internal instruments to go haywire, forcing most travel to be on foot or mounted on the local beasts. ### Befriending The Foal

The crate can either be opened partially, allowing the characters to see and interact with (and feed!) the foal, or they can fully release the creature from its “containment unit.” Unless they choose to leave the foal fully contained as they head out, the first character(s) it interacts with outside of the crate must make a DC 16 Wisdom (Animal Handling) check. If the character(s) know the Celestial language, they can make a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check instead. In either case, the check is made at Advantage if the foal is offered food of any kind. On a failure, the foal falls into a woeful state and occasionally lets out a mournful, hollow groan that adds a negative impact to the encounter found at the bridge over Redsmoke Gorge (see “Bridge Over Redsmoke Gorge” further in this adventure). If the check is successful, the foal takes quite kindly to the characters involved and will constantly rub against them (if possible), hum and clack for attention, and generally bond. This grants a bonus towards a possible non-violent encounter at Redsmoke Gorge (see “Bridge Over Redsmoke Gorge” further in this adventure), as well as allow the characters to reroll any dice used to determine the regaining of Hit Points from the Ebon Unicorn’s Aura of Vigor.

#### EXPLAIN EVERYTHING

The characters can try to delay Stormnear’s aggression by explaining how they have been protecting the foal and trying to deliver it to a safe place in the Unicorn Forest. If the foal is instead mourning its captivity, the check is at Disadvantage. If the second check fails (or if they refuse to release the foal), they must defend themselves (see below) instead!

#### HAND THE FOAL OVER

If the characters simply release the foal into Stormnear’s custody, it will happily join its father. Stormnear will dismiss his Unicorn brethren and accompany the characters to their rendezvous with Franket on the opposite side of the bridge. The Ebon Unicorn will give the courier a chance to explain himself (truthfully!) as to why the foal was being taken to Willa and the Unicorn Grove.

#### DEFEND THEMSELVES

Some characters may simply decide to fight off the unicorns. This is the most direct, if not the least diplomatic, way of dealing with things. It is also the most deadly, as even though they’re young, these unicorns are a challenge for the characters. Throughout the combat, the foal will try to break free and escape to the side of his father, at which point the unicorns will collectively use their Word of Recall spells to return to their grove. No matter how this encounter ends, the characters will have earned the ire of Franket, Willa, the warrior maidens, and the unicorns!

### Adventure Background

Miraculously, Thundera has reformed, and Mumm-Ra has already entrenched himself upon the reborn world. Wielding the dreaded Sword of Plun-Darr, he searches relentlessly for the legendary Treasure of Thundera, now shattered and scattered across the planet. ThunderCats forces are stretched thin between Third Earth and New Thundera as they race to recover the fragments before he does while still facing off against the threats on Third Earth. In the Valley of the Snarfs, the Snarfs discovered one such piece. Not long afterwards, however, Captain Shiner’s spaceship, the Vertus, descended upon the Valley and launched a sudden assault, seizing the piece of the Treasure. Before communications were cut, the snarfs were able to send a distress message to the ThunderCats describing the attack and warning that Shiner’s forces had occupied the Valley. No word has come from them since. Unknown to the ThunderCats, Mumm-Ra has exploited their divided attention across two worlds by hiring Captain Shiner to retrieve the piece of the Treasure for him. Shiner’s mercenary forces are too numerous for a direct assault in the ThunderCats’ current state. A small team of skilled infiltrators must liberate the Valley and secretly board the Vertus to reclaim the piece of the Treasure before Captain Shiner can hand it off to Mumm-Ra. PERSONALIZING THE ADVENTURE To strengthen player investment and add greater emotional weight to the story, consider having one or more of the characters have friends among the snarfs of the Valley from their backstories.

The ThunderCats have stretched themselves thin searching the wild and ever-changing landscapes of their restored homeworld, and you have done the same. ### Valley of the Snarfs

Nestled among Thundera’s rolling lowlands, the Valley of the Snarfs was once a quiet haven where the snarfs lived simple, contented lives. Cozy homes dotted the winding paths, and every garden bloomed under their care. Life was peaceful, until the destruction of Thundera forced the snarfs to flee and seek refuge on the Planet of Snarfs. Miraculously, when Thundera was reborn, the valley endured. Much of it remains intact, a rare remnant of the world that was. Many snarfs have now returned to once again tend their homes and gardens.

### Area Information

The Valley of the Snarfs has the following features. Dimensions and Terrain. The buildings in the valley are crafted from bright stone and crowned with tall, pointed roofs of warm colors. Raised pathways carved directly from natural rock wind between structures, dotted with patches of green growth while lush trees fill nearly every open space. Towering mountains surround the settlement and loom over the valley. In the aftermath of the recent attack, several buildings lie partially collapsed and scattered fires continue to burn throughout the area. Lighting. The sloping walls of the valley and the encircling mountains limit direct sunlight, leaving much of the town in Dim Light or Darkness for most of the day. Only a brief window of Bright Light reaches the settlement before shadows reclaim it. At present, the flames left behind by Shiner’s assault cast a harsh and flickering glow across many sections of the valley. Smells and Sounds. Wind moves steadily through the valley, whistling across stone surfaces and stirring the dense vegetation. The town’s usual fresh and earthy scents are now overwhelmed by thick smoke, and the crackle of burning structures mingles with the restless rush of air.

### Shiner’s Soldiers

There is a Shiner Soldier (see further in this adventure) for every character patrolling the Valley. They are actively searching for any hostiles, giving them a +5 to their Passive Perception, making it an 18. The snarfs are barricaded inside their homes and businesses. At the first sign of trouble, the soldiers use the communicators built into their uniforms to contact the Vertus and raise an alert. It is crucial that this does not happen so the characters can maintain stealth and complete their mission with ease. Their Short-Range Signal Jammers can easily accomplish this. If the soldiers are able to send an alarm, the Vertus is put on High Alert (see further in this adventure) and the characters are bound to have a much more difficult task ahead of them.

STEALTH MISSION Players need to understand that Captain Shiner’s forces will easily overwhelm them if they attempt a full assault or raise the alarm. This first encounter is an ideal way to show that. Handling the soldiers should not be too much trouble, but narration and context should make it clear that a larger force would be a serious threat. Be sure to remind the players about their Short-Range Signal Jammer, as it will be critical in keeping them from facing Shiner’s entire army. It has a finite number of uses, so they should avoid overusing it whenever possible, but in many situations it will be the only thing standing between them and Shiner’s full wrath. You may need to slow things down occasionally to give the players time to react. Perhaps it takes a round before the soldiers use their communicators. Maybe they focus on the characters first and only use the communicators if the situation starts to go badly. Keep things realistic and fair, but remember that stealth is the key to this adventure and once it is lost it cannot be regained. Uniforms. The soldiers’ armored uniforms (Half Plate Armor) can be taken to be worn as an assist with the upcoming infiltration of the Vertus, especially to overhear any communication between soldiers. However, this armor is specifically crafted for their wearers and not designed for a wide array of species. While wearing them will help deter any immediate hostilities, they won’t be of much help under close scrutiny.

RUNNING THE STEALTH MISSION As implied earlier, this adventure is built around stealth, so expect to improvise as players develop unexpected plans and solutions. Reward clever thinking while maintaining tension and forward momentum. Characters may possess abilities or resources that make sneaking significantly easier. A party where everyone can cast Invisibility, for instance, will naturally bypass many obstacles. Rather than immediately countering these advantages with perfect defenses, let the world react gradually. Soldiers might notice doors opening without cause, hear unexplained movement, or catch an unfamiliar scent lingering in a corridor. Suspicion should grow before direct countermeasures appear. Once the alarm is truly raised, enemy forces can adapt by equipping tools such as thermographic or ultrasound vision that allow them to challenge invisible intruders.

### Conclusion

EVIL WINS? Note that Mumm-Ra is capable of seizing the flag before the players can. He can cast Wall of Force to block pursuit, destroy the display case and claim the flag on his following turn, then use Teleport to escape on the turn after. Obviously, this wouldn’t be a great finale for the players to experience, so make sure the players have meaningful ways to interfere. Characters with teleportation traits, features, or magic such as Misty Step can bypass a Wall of Force, but if the party has no realistic way to overcome that spell, consider removing it from Mumm-Ra’s stat block for this encounter. That said, stakes only matter if failure is possible. If the players were unable to stop Mumm-Ra or chose not to focus on him, allow him to escape with the flag. Let their frustration fuel the story by directing it toward Mumm-Ra himself and encouraging a future rematch (perhaps with “Mumm-Ra Must Die!” later in this book). Once the characters obtain the Ancient ThunderCats Flag (or Mumm-Ra makes off with it), they must escape the Vertus. Snarf Oswald, if he has not yet been rescued, will also need to be freed (see area V7). How difficult this is depends on the choices the characters have made up to this point. Below are some suggested outcomes. Like A Ghost. If the characters raised no suspicion and avoided any actions that might draw attention, leaving should be relatively simple. Freeing Snarf Oswald could complicate matters. They can return to the shuttle they used to arrive or more likely use an escape pod (area V5). Guns Blazing. If the Vertus is on High Alert (see “High Alert”), the characters will need to make a break for it. Each time they enter a new area, roll a d6. A roll other than 6 indicates a patrol (see “Patrols”). Characters should focus on escape rather than fighting, likely reaching an escape pod (area V5) since the shuttle will be heavily guarded. Freeing Snarf Oswald at this point will be a serious challenge. Causing a reactor meltdown (area V14) would stop all patrols, making it much easier to free Snarf Oswald and get out if not put a serious time crunch on doing so. Just Good Business. If the characters handed Scorpius’ journal (see area V19) to Shiner and Scorpius had not yet arrived, they’ll need to navigate the ship as the two factions battle each other (see area V21). Consider requiring saving throws and skill checks to maneuver through the firefights to either the shuttle or the escape pods (area V5). If Scorpius had arrived and Shiner fought him with the characters, Shiner lets the characters leave unharmed in thanks for their help in stopping the traitorous soldier.

### Back To Base

Once the characters escape the Vertus, they can return to New Cats’ Lair. With The Flag and Snarf Oswald. If they recovered the flag and freed Snarf Oswald, the ThunderCats will give them a hero’s welcome. With The Flag But Not Snarf Oswald. If the characters recovered the flag but could not save Snarf Oswald, Snarf Eggbert will be inconsolable and even Snarf himself will be far less chipper. The ThunderCats will honor Oswald’s sacrifice and vow to rescue him someday. If Oswald went down with the ship, they hold a funeral in his honor. No Flag But With Snarf Oswald. ### What’s Next?

If this adventure serves as part of an ongoing campaign, the characters may choose to continue based on their ending, returning for Snarf Oswald or chasing after Mumm-Ra for example. The story could also expand with Scorpius, who turns out to be an eldritch being older than even Mumm-Ra, able to regenerate from his failed coup and seek revenge on the characters. For a self-contained one-shot, however, escaping the Vertus marks the natural end of the adventure. If you are including this adventure in an ongoing campaign with the other adventures in this book, advance the characters to level 9.

### Adventure Background

With the mu’tants of Plun-Darr and the Lunataks banished to Exile Isle and no longer threatening Third Earth, the ThunderCats have mostly departed to focus on restoring their renewed homeworld of New Thundera. To prevent Third Earth from being left defenseless, they worked with the locals to create the League of Third Earth. Not all threats have been removed, however. The Berserkers continue to roam Third Earth, and with most of the ThunderCats now offworld, their raids have become bolder and more ruthless. They struck without warning against the peaceful nai village on the edge of the Northern Ocean, seizing all the village’s bounty and establishing themselves as oppressive lords over its people. The nai have sent a representative, Drella, to plead with the League of Third Earth for help in reclaiming their stolen treasures. Now the League must assemble a strike team to confront the Berserkers and put an end to their plundering before more innocent people fall under their cyborg boots. What neither the League nor the Berserkers realize is that deep beneath the Northern Ocean sleeps an eldritch titan named Scylla. Generations of nai pacifism have kept her dormant, but the Berserkers’ violence has begun to stir her. Any further aggression in the region could fully awaken her and unleash her wrath on all nearby.

### The Berserkers

This ruthless band of cybernetic pirates plunders the seas of Third Earth under the banner of Hammerhand, choosing a life of violence and conflict, which soon had everyone referring to them by a fitting moniker: the Berserkers. Since most of the ThunderCats departed Third Earth, Hammerhand has replenished his cybernetically-enhanced crew with new warriors, and they have resumed their marauding mercenary ways at top speed, eager to fill their coffers and hold with ill-gotten gains. The Berserkers live by a simple creed of might makes right, seeing anything weaker than themselves as theirs to conquer and dominate. PERSONALIZING THE ADVENTURE To strengthen player investment and add greater emotional weight to the story, consider having one or more of the characters have history with the Berserkers or nai from their backstories. Alternatively, if you’re running this adventure as part of an ongoing campaign using this book, you can reference the party’s last run-in with the Berserkers in Thorburn (see Chapter 2).

### The Adventure Begins

When the adventure begins, the characters are on their way to the nai coastal village in a provided vehicle after being given their assignment by the League of Third Earth. Read or paraphrase the following: With the mu’tants and Lunataks imprisoned and Mumm-Ra banished from Third Earth, the ThunderCats have turned their focus to reclaiming New Thundera. To safeguard peace in their absence, they established the League of Third Earth, made up of local inhabitants and a representative of the Interplanetary Control Force. The League is charged with maintaining order across the unified northern hemisphere of the planet. As such, when a terrified and haggard nai, a pinkskinned aquatic species native to the remote coastal regions of Third Earth, arrived at the League speaking of Berserker raids, they acted without hesitation. It seems that with most of the ThunderCats now offworld, the Berserkers had grown bolder in their attacks. The nai, who introduced herself as Drella, explained: “I was with my brother Prin, and we were just drawing in our nets when fire arced from the sky and struck among us. We were stunned, frightened. We didn’t know what to do. The Berserkers, of course, knew exactly what to do. As the rising sun broke, they attacked with fury. We had no defense. The Berserkers drove us from our homes. We were like bird-fish fleeing a school of hungry sharks. At first, we thought the Berserkers might leave, once they looted our town. They did not. They stayed, tracked us like animals, caught us, and made us work for them. In desperation, our village elders arranged my escape, and now I seek your aid. Even in my remote land, we’ve heard of the League of Third Earth and the noble ThunderCats. If anyone can rescue us, you can! Please help us!” So it was that you were chosen to travel to the northern coast and put an end to the Berserkers. Drella has guided you for days, and now as you crest the final hill, the humble village lies ahead. Off the shore, the unmistakable sight of a Berserker warship bobs in the water, waiting like a predator for any who dares approach.

Once the characters have had a chance to understand the situation and play out their initial character moments, they will likely want to get their bearings.

Perched on the edge of the Northern Ocean and cradled by coral reefs, the nai live as peaceful fisherfolk. They are gentle by nature and refuse to wield weapons, believing that violence is wrong no matter the cost.

### Adventure Background

New Thundera has been fully restored and all corrupting influence has been removed. Refugees continue to arrive daily, bringing hope for the future. In response, the ThunderCats have formed the ThunderGuard, training a new generation of heroes to protect what they have rebuilt. Now the ThunderGuard and the ThunderCats face the greatest challenge they have ever encountered. MummRa’s plotting has finally come to light. Every corpse on New Thundera, from the Thunderians who lived before the planet’s destruction to the mu’tants who fell in the great wars, has risen under his dark power and now threatens all that the ThunderCats have fought to restore. ### Mumm-Ra the Ever-Living

Mumm-Ra is a mummified, ancient, and immortal devil-priest of Third Earth who serves the Ancient Spirits of Evil. Though his emaciated body seems frail, he wields powerful dark magic and countless cunning tricks. Once a sem priest named Wahankh in ancient Egypt, he discovered power stones that connected him to the Ancient Spirits of Evil. Through a dark pact, he gained immense magic, his flesh became necrotic and immortal, and he was reborn as Mumm-Ra. He tried to overthrow the pharaoh but was entombed in the Onyx Pyramid, forced to labor for the spirits while they corrupted the tomb into a locus of their power. Centuries later he reemerged to claim Third Earth, only to be driven back by the Rammastide uprising. He rested, letting legends of his evil fade as the peoples of Third Earth drifted apart.

When the ThunderCats and mu’tants arrived, Mumm-Ra awoke. He seized the chance to reclaim his power, temporarily allied with the mu’tants, and sought the Eye of Thundera. Over time he grew stronger, acquiring deadly artifacts like the Sword of Plun-Darr and creating a New Onyx Pyramid on New Thundera. When the ThunderCats completed the ritual of the Book of Omens and purified New Thundera from all corrupting influence, Mumm-Ra receded once again, only to now finally reveal his ultimate plan. #### SECRET ENTRANCE

There is a secret door that leads into the pyramid on the ground level, but it is well-hidden. ### Resting

Mumm-Ra’s lair is as dangerous as one would expect, making resting a risky proposition. There are a few rooms where a Short Rest can be taken safely once the immediate threats are eliminated. A Long Rest is far more difficult, as New Cats’ Lair would need to defend against the undead onslaught for the entire duration, a task that is likely to be lethal. For heroes determined to succeed, a Long Rest is simply not an option.

#### O10. SECRET CHAMBER

The secret chamber behind Mumm-Ra’s sarcophagus is only 30-by-30 feet in size with a 60-foot ceiling. This chamber is used by Mumm-Ra to store powerful artifacts he wants to keep out of the hands of rivals and also as a place to recuperate from combat in his sarcophagus chamber (area O9). Ancient Statues of Evil. The statues in area O9 can’t enter this room as the secret door is too small for them. Mumm-Ra The All-Powerful. This is where the final battle is likely to take place. It will be a harrowing fight, but should the characters prove victorious, Mumm-Ra will scream in disbelief and dissolve into grave dust. STATUE FIGHT The Ancient Statues of Evil are resistant or outright immune to most forms of damage and also possess the Magic Resistance trait. That is because they are not intended to be defeated in the traditional way. Instead, the statues are designed to destroy each other using their Animated Stone and Siege Monster traits along with Slam attacks that bypass Resistances and Immunities. Consider guiding players into noticing this early, as once they see that most of their options have little effect, they may otherwise feel frustrated or cheated. Alternatively, because the statues cannot enter area O10, the players may choose to flee from them and confront Mumm-Ra. The statues will try to make Opportunity Attacks if they can, but otherwise this is a viable option. Keep in mind that choosing this path will prevent the New Onyx Pyramid from collapsing, though the players will likely not know that.

BOSS BATTLE The fight with Mumm-Ra, Ma-Mutt, and the Ancient Statues of Evil should feel overwhelming in the best possible way. There is a very real chance of failure, and that risk keeps the tension high and the victory meaningful. One of the greatest challenges is Mumm-Ra’s Evil Rejuvenation trait. Consider leaning into his arrogance. He may gloat that he is ever-living so long as his sarcophagus endures, bragging about it even before his first revival. He might also boast that the boiling fluid within his cauldron can halt any magic. This can serve as a subtle clue that the cauldron could be used to suppress the sarcophagus’ power. Because this is a game of dice, the momentum of battle can shift quickly. If Mumm-Ra and his forces are crushing the party, consider removing his Mumm-Ra the All-Powerful form from the encounter to keep the challenge palpable but maintain a sense of possible success for the players. If the fight feels too easy, raise the stakes. Swarms of Zombies can begin shambling into the sarcophagus chamber or emerging from hidden passages, forcing the characters to divide their focus and fight on multiple fronts.

### Conclusion

Once Mumm-Ra the All-Powerful falls, a heavy silence settles over the New Onyx Pyramid. If they still remain, the Ancient Statues of Evil grind back to their original positions and fall dormant once more, and Ma-Mutt dissipates into black mist. The risen dead of Thundera dissolve into drifting grave dust. The spell is broken. The day is won.

### Collapsing Pyramid

If the New Onyx Pyramid is in the midst of collapse (see area O9), the characters must flee for their lives. Each character makes a DC 16 Strength (Athletics), Dexterity (Acrobatics), Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation) check to navigate the crumbling labyrinth and reach one of the exits. Clever tactics, strong teamwork, or creative use of spells and equipment can grant Advantage on the roll. On a success, the character stays just ahead of the destruction as falling stone crashes down at their heels. On a failure, the character is trapped beneath rubble within the dying pyramid. A character who succeeds by 5 or more may turn back to rescue a trapped ally, making the check again. If this second check fails, both characters are caught in the collapse.

### The End Of

### Mumm-Ra?

If the pyramid has collapsed, Mumm-Ra’s corruption is finally purged from New Thundera. If Mumm-Ra The All-Powerful was defeated but the pyramid still stands, the devil-priest may yet find a path back to power. For now, however, his dark design lies in ruins. The surviving heroes limp back to their battered aircraft, see it repaired, and return at last to New Cats’ Lair, where a thunderous cheer greets them.

### What Comes Next

If this adventure serves as part of an ongoing campaign, the characters might turn their attention to reclaiming Grimalkingrad. If the pyramid remains standing, they may seek a way to destroy it forever. It is even possible that Mumm-Ra set a final scheme in motion, one that awakens only in the moment of his defeat. For a self-contained one-shot, however, the defeat of Mumm-Ra and the ending of his foul sorcery marks the natural end of the adventure. If you are including this adventure in an ongoing campaign with the other adventures in this book, the characters’ stories have concluded for now.
